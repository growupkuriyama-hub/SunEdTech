Why does water expand so much when it becomes steam?

You've probably heard that water becomes roughly 1,700 times larger when it turns into steam. That number is real—under specific conditions.

GROWUP EXPLAINS·Nov 13, 2026·5 MIN READ
ONE GRAM / TWO VERY DIFFERENT VOLUMES
1 mL → ~1.7 L
Liquid water → water vapor, near 100°C and 1 atm

First: 1,700 times at what temperature and pressure?

Volume is not an intrinsic fixed property of a gas. Gas volume changes with temperature and pressure. The “1,700 times” figure is an approximate comparison between liquid water and water vapor near 100°C at one atmosphere.

Let's calculate it from one gram

One milliliter of liquid water has a mass of approximately one gram. That same gram contains the same number of H₂O molecules after boiling.

01

Molar mass of water: 18.0 g/mol.

02

One gram is approximately 1/18.0 mol.

03

At 100°C (373 K) and 1 atm, the ideal gas law gives a molar volume of 30.6 L/mol.

04

So one gram of steam occupies 30.6/18.0 ≈ 1.70 L.

THE VOLUME RATIO1,700 mL ÷ 1 mL ≈ 1,700

That is where the famous multiplier comes from. It is an approximation, not a universal conversion factor.

What's changing at the molecular level?

In the liquid, water molecules remain close together. In a gas at atmospheric pressure, molecules are much farther apart on average. The molecules themselves don't expand 1,700 times. The empty space between them increases enormously.

Does steam always have a temperature of 100°C?

No. Water boils around 100°C only at approximately 1 atm. At lower pressure it boils at a lower temperature; under higher pressure, at a higher one. After it forms, water vapor can also be heated above its boiling temperature—called superheated steam.

There's another common misconception: the white cloud you see above a kettle is mainly tiny liquid droplets formed when invisible water vapor cools and condenses.

✳
The important idea

Converting liquid water to vapor doesn't create more H₂O molecules. It gives the same molecules vastly more room. Always specify temperature and pressure when quoting gas volume.
