QUESTION 01 / THE LANGUAGE OF ELECTRONS

In your own words, explain oxidation and reduction in terms of electron transfer. Can both processes occur in the same reaction?

Show the worked answer

Oxidation is the loss of electrons; reduction is the gain of electrons. They take place together in a redox reaction: electrons lost by one species must be accepted by another.

QUESTION 06 / OXIDATION NUMBERS

Calculate the oxidation number of sulfur in H₂SO₄. Show your equation.

Show the worked answer

Let sulfur's oxidation number be x. With hydrogen at +1 and oxygen at −2, the neutral molecule gives 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0. Solving gives x = +6.

QUESTION 11 / HALF-EQUATIONS

Write the oxidation half-equation for Zn(s) changing to Zn²⁺(aq).

Show the worked answer

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻. The two electrons appear on the product side because oxidation involves losing electrons.

QUESTION 22 / ACIDS AND METALS

What normally happens when copper metal is placed in ordinary dilute sulfuric acid? Explain using the role of H⁺.

Show the worked answer

Under ordinary conditions there is no appreciable reaction. H⁺ is not a sufficiently effective oxidizing partner to convert copper metal to Cu²⁺ while forming H₂. Copper lies below hydrogen in the familiar activity series.

QUESTION 27 / THINK LIKE A CHEMIST

Compare the gases produced by (a) Zn + dilute H₂SO₄ and (b) Cu + hot concentrated H₂SO₄. Why are they different?

Show the worked answer

(a) H₂: hydrogen ions accept electrons from zinc. (b) SO₂: sulfur(VI) in hot concentrated sulfuric acid is reduced to sulfur(IV). Under these different conditions, different species accept electrons.
